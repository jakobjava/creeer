# ADR-0037: Sound from the simulation

- **Status:** Built (G2.11 stream A7, 2026-11-13/14): synthesis, mixer, device output, offline renderer and the client's hooks; waiting on the user's ears at the check-in.
- **Context:** the game was silent (`creeer_audio` was an empty crate). The user, 2026-11-13: the game is "not fun"; the first thing that must be fun is a bridge built and loaded until it breaks (`docs/playability-plan.md`). There are no recorded sounds and nobody to record them, and the project's first principle is that the simulation answers what it can (plan.md, Phase B principle 1).
- **Builds on:** the materials (`data/materials/engineering.ron`: E, G, ρ, loss factor), the bodies the client is told (`creeer_net::entity::Kind`, `Matter`), the snapshots' velocities (`creeer_net::quant::BodyState`), the broken members (`ServerMessage::Plastic`), the terrain's rock at a point (`Terrain::rock_at`), the feet of the animation (ADR-0026 decision 9).
- **Evidence:** `crates/creeer_audio` (synthesis, mixer, device, offline renderer, analysis; tests beside each part), `data/sound/` (the sound attributes), `crates/creeer_client/src/sounds*.rs` (the hooks) and `sound_render.rs` (the `--sound` and `--sound-play` modes). The rendered sounds and their measurements: the stream's frames folder, `sound/` (`sounds.md` and the WAVs; rendered again by `creeer_client --sound <dir>`).

## Prior work

Surveyed 2026-11-13: papers on physically based sound, middleware and shipped games, and what is known to synthesise badly.

| Work | What it does | Results and limits | For Creëer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **van den Doel & Pai**, "The sounds of physical shapes" (Presence 7(4), 1998) | A struck object's sound is a sum of damped sinusoids, y(t) = Σ aₙ e^(−dₙt) sin(2πfₙt): frequencies from the shape and material (bars, plates, membranes), gains from the mode shapes at the point struck, decays dₙ = π fₙ tan φ from the material's internal friction φ (after Wildes & Richards 1988, who showed tan φ alone lets listeners tell wood, glass and metal apart) | Real time on 1998 hardware; convincing for bars and plates; the radiation is left out (gains by ear) | **Taken:** the modal model, the bar's modes, the strike point's gains, the decay law from the material's loss factor |
| **van den Doel, Kry & Pai**, "FoleyAutomatic" (SIGGRAPH 2001) | Impacts, scraping and rolling from a rigid-body simulation: modal resonators excited by contact force models. An impact's force is a short pulse (its length from the contact's stiffness); scraping is a fractal surface profile read at the slip speed; rolling the same profile filtered by the contact's size | Hundreds of modes in real time; the contact force models are what makes it sound right, not the mode count | **Taken:** the force pulse of an impact (its length from Hertz, below), scraping and rolling as filtered noise read at the speed, through the body's modes |
| **O'Brien, Shen & Gatchalian**, "Synthesizing sounds from rigid-body simulations" (SCA 2002); **James, Barbič & Pai**, "Precomputed acoustic transfer" (SIGGRAPH 2006) | Modes from a finite-element model of any shape; how each mode radiates, precomputed | Exact for arbitrary shapes; minutes to hours of precomputation per shape | **Not taken now:** our parts are bars, boxes and logs, whose modes are analytic; any shape's FEM modes come when parts of any shape do (future improvements) |
| **Raghuvanshi & Lin**, "Interactive sound synthesis for large scale environments" (I3D 2006) | Modal synthesis for hundreds of objects at once: modes too quiet to hear dropped, modes closer than the ear can tell apart merged, quality scaled to a budget | Hundreds of sounding objects at over 100 frames a second | **Taken:** a mode is synthesised only if it is loud enough to hear; a voice limit, the quietest voice giving way |
| **Bonneel, Drettakis, Tsingos, Viaud-Delmon & James**, "Fast modal sounds with scalable frequency-domain synthesis" (SIGGRAPH 2008) | Modes synthesised in the frequency domain, 5–8× faster | Pays above thousands of modes | **Not taken:** at our voice limit (32 voices of at most 24 modes) a time-domain resonator costs a few per cent of a core (Cost) |
| **Raghuvanshi, Lloyd & Govindaraju**, "Sound synthesis for impact sounds in video games" (I3D 2011; shipped in *Crackdown 2*, Xbox 360) | Modes fitted to a recorded impact, varied at run time | Less memory than recordings; better with the recorded envelopes than with pure exponentials | Confirms modal impacts ship; we have no recordings to fit, so the modes come from the physics |
| **Zheng & James**, "Rigid-body fracture sound with precomputed soundbanks" (SIGGRAPH 2010) | Fracture as many small impacts and the pieces' own ringing | Offline; the debris' modes and the "crackle" of the break matter most | **Taken in its simplest form:** a break is a burst of micro-fractures through the pieces' modes |
| **Sethna, Dahmen & Myers**, "Crackling noise" (Nature 410, 2001); **Salminen, Tolvanen & Alava**, "Acoustic emission from paper fracture" (PRL 89, 2002) | Materials breaking emit bursts whose energies follow a power law (exponent about 1.3 for paper) | Measured over decades of energy | **Taken:** a crack's micro-fractures have power-law energies |
| **Acoustic emission of timber under load** (UBC thesis on bending specimens; University of Bath, softwood in tension; Ayarkwa et al., finger joints; J. Wood Sci. 2005) | Wood emits small cracking events as it is loaded: few at first, a rate change near the proportional limit, a steep rise just before failure; used to predict a member's strength | Qualitative onset ("a marked increase in count rate at the proportional limit"); counts at 80 % of the failure stress predict strength | **Taken:** a loaded member creaks at a rate that rises steeply as its stress nears its strength (onset near half of it, steep past 80 %) |
| **Avanzini, Serafin & Rocchesso**, "Interactive simulation of rigid body interaction with friction-induced sound generation" (IEEE TSAP 13(5), 2005) | An elasto-plastic friction model coupling modal resonators: rubbing, braking, squeaky doors | Real time on ordinary computers | **Adapted:** a creak is stick–slip: a train of slips at an irregular rate through the member's modes; the friction model itself is not integrated (too costly per voice, and the slip rate is what the ear follows) |
| **Cook**, "Physically informed sonic modeling (PhISEM)" (Computer Music Journal 21(3), 1997); "Modeling Bill's gait" (AES 22nd conference, 2002) | Shakers, gravel and footsteps as many particles colliding at random, their rate following an energy envelope, each collision through a few resonances | A handful of parameters per surface; footsteps analysed into heel and toe events | **Taken:** footsteps on loose ground as grains colliding, their rate following the step's force (heel, then toe), through the ground's grain resonances |
| **Fontana & Bresin**, "Physics-based sound synthesis and control: crushing, walking and running by crumpling sounds" (2003); **Turchet, Serafin, Dimitrov & Nordahl**, "Physically based sound synthesis and control of footsteps sounds" (DAFx 2010) | Footsteps on gravel, snow and dry leaves as crumpling: micro-impacts of power-law energies driven by the ground reaction force | Listeners recognise the surfaces | Confirms Cook's approach; the ground reaction force drives the grains |
| **Koss & Alfredson**, "Transient sound radiated by spheres undergoing an elastic collision" (J. Sound Vib. 27, 1973); **Akay**, "A review of impact noise" (JASA 64, 1978) | Two kinds of impact sound: the "acceleration noise" of the bodies stopped in the contact time, and the ringing of their modes | The click's loudness follows the contact time: the shorter, the louder and brighter | **Taken:** a short click from the contact pulse itself, besides the modes |
| **Hertz contact** (Johnson, *Contact Mechanics*, 1985, §11.4) | The time two elastic bodies touch: t = 2.87 (m*² / (R* E*² v))^(1/5) | Exact for elastic spheres | **Taken:** the force pulse's length from the two surfaces' E*, the bodies' masses, the contact's radius and the closing speed: stone on stone clicks, wood on soil thuds |
| **Fahy & Gardonio**, *Sound and Structural Vibration* (2007) | How a vibrating surface radiates: a baffled piston's pressure ρ₀ω S v / (2π r); an unbaffled narrow strip radiates far less below the wavelength of its width (the "acoustic short circuit") | Standard engineering acoustics | **Taken, simplified:** each mode radiates as a piston of the body's face, reduced by k·w while the face is narrower than the wavelength |
| **ISO 9613-1** (air absorption) | Air absorbs high frequencies with distance: about 0.02 dB/m at 4 kHz, 0.08 dB/m at 8 kHz (20 °C) | Standard | **Taken:** distance dulls a sound (a low-pass whose corner falls with distance), and sound arrives at 343 m/s (a hammer seen far off is heard later) |
| **Farnell**, *Designing Sound* (MIT Press 2010); **Fournel**, "Procedural audio for video games: are we there yet?" (GDC 2010) | Procedural models in Pure Data for wind, creaking doors, footsteps, fire, engines; the case for procedural audio in games | Wind as noise shaped by gusts is the standard model; procedural audio's weakness is the time to design each model | **Taken:** wind as turbulent noise with gusts, its speed from the wind's log profile at the listener's height |
| **Dobashi, Yamamoto & Nishita**, "Real-time rendering of aerodynamic sound" (SIGGRAPH 2003) | Aeolian tones of wind past thin objects (Strouhal: f ≈ 0.2 U/D) from CFD-built sound textures | Real time | Later: members whistling in the wind (future improvements) |
| **Shipped games and middleware** | *GTA V* (an in-house synthesis toolkit, MacGregor, GDC 2014: engines, ambience); *No Man's Sky* (VocAlien, physically modelled creature voices, Weir, GDC 2017); *Peggle Blast* (sounds synthesised in Wwise, 1.3 MB of effects); *Crackdown 2* (modal impacts, above); Audiokinetic's SoundSeed (Impact: modal resynthesis; Air: wind and whooshes) | Procedural audio ships where the sound follows the game's state closely (engines, wind, impacts); voices, music and creatures are still mostly recorded | **Taken:** impacts, wind and the sounds the physics drives are synthesised |
| **What synthesises badly** (the surveys above; Fournel 2010; Farnell 2010) | Speech and voices, music, animals, liquids (they need bubble models: van den Doel 2005, Moss et al. 2010), fire, crowds, rooms' reverberation without a model of the room; synthesised sounds are often judged "thin" when too few modes are kept or the contact is too clean | | None of these is in G2's bridge; water and fire come with their gameplay (future improvements) |

**Engines and libraries for the output.** `kira` 0.12 (MIT or Apache-2.0; plan.md §2) plays sounds through `cpal` on every platform, mixes them on tracks, and lets a game add its own source by the `Sound` trait (`process(&mut self, out: &mut [Frame], dt, info)`, allocation-free). `cpal` alone would need the stream callback, the sample formats and device loss handled by hand. `rodio` is built for playing files. Unity, Unreal and FMOD offer synthesis only as plugins (SoundSeed, Unreal's MetaSounds graphs). **Taken:** kira with only its `cpal` backend (no file decoders), our whole mixer as one kira `Sound`.

## Decision

1. **Everything heard is synthesised from the simulation.** There are no recorded sounds. A sound's frequencies, decays, brightness and loudness come from what the physics knows: the material's E, G, ρ and loss factor, the body's shape and size, the impulse, the closing speed, the stored strain energy. What the physics does not know is a **sound attribute in `data/sound/`**, with its source or marked as an estimate.
2. **Struck bodies ring at their own modes** (van den Doel & Pai). A slender body (longer than three times its thickness) is a free–free Euler–Bernoulli bar along its longest axis: bending modes in both planes, fₙ = (βₙL)²/(2πL²)·√(EI/(ρA)) with βₙL = 4.730, 7.853, 10.996, … and the longitudinal modes fₙ = n·√(E/ρ)/(2L). A compact body is a sphere of its volume: its torsional modes, kₛa the roots of x·j′ₙ(x) = jₙ(x) (Lamb 1882), with kₛ = 2πf/√(G/ρ). A nail is a cantilever of its standing length (β₁L = 1.875, …): its pitch rises as it is driven in. Each mode decays at dₙ = π fₙ η (η the loss factor). Each mode's gain is the mode's shape at the struck point times the impulse's component along it, over the body's mass.
3. **Contact sets the brightness and the click** (Hertz; Koss & Alfredson). An impact is a half-sine force pulse whose length is Hertz's contact time from the two surfaces' E*, the reduced mass, the contact radius and the closing speed; a mode above 1/(2τ) is excited by less (the pulse's spectrum envelope 1/(1 + (2fτ)²)). A short pulse also makes a click.
4. **Radiation** (Fahy): each mode radiates the power ρ₀c·S·⟨v²⟩·σ of its face (area S, velocity v), spread over a sphere: p(1 m) = ρ₀c·v·√(S·σ/4π), its efficiency σ = (kw)²/(1 + (kw)²) for a face w wide (a narrow face loses its sound round its edges below its wavelength). The click of a body stopped short is ρ₀c·a/2 per m/s of its velocity's change (a its size), passed by its contact's low-pass (corner 1/(πτ)) and a high-pass at c/(2πa): of second order in open air (a dipole, Koss & Alfredson), of first order against the ground (the ground's patch moves with it: a piston in a baffle). **Distance:** 1/r, air absorption (ISO 9613-1, as a low-pass whose corner falls as 1/√r), arrival at 343 m/s. **Direction:** equal-power stereo panning, the far ear never below a third. **Audibility:** a sound below the threshold of hearing at the listener, or 60 dB below the loudest heard, is not begun; nothing further than the hearing range (300 m) is gathered.
5. **Events, most important first** (each from what the client already has; the protocol unchanged):
   - **Impacts and landings:** from the snapshots' velocities (told with every body, 30 a second): a body whose velocity changed between two snapshots by more than holding it up against gravity gives it (g·dt) was struck, with impulse m·Δv; what struck it is the ground (its rock) or the nearest body or member within 0.3 m. Its material is the engineering material nearest its Young's modulus and density (both told: `Matter`), a felled part's its species' wood, a structure's member the material `hearing.ron` names for structures.
   - **Tool strikes:** the player's own swing at its blow, on what the crosshair is on; **driving a fastener:** the blows of its driver (`data/sound/drivers.ron`: a claw hammer for nails, a mallet for pegs) over the fastening's time, each on the fastener and the part, the nail's standing length shortening blow by blow.
   - **A member breaking:** the newly broken members (`ServerMessage::Plastic`), or, for a structure come apart (told as its members replaced by fewer, its pieces new entities), the largest member gone: a burst of micro-fractures (power-law energies, the material's crack duration) through the two pieces' modes, its energy the strain energy the member held at its strength times the share that becomes vibration (estimate).
   - **A loaded member creaking:** stick–slip through the member's modes at a rate rising with its utilisation (σ/f) as timber's acoustic emission does. *The client is not told a member's stress*: the synthesis and its API are built and tested, and fed by the server's own world in the offline scenes; in play it waits on a protocol addition (Limits).
   - **Footsteps:** each foot's landing (the animation's), by the ground under it: loose ground as grains (Cook), rock and timber as the sole's impact through the ground's or the member's modes.
   - **Rolling and scraping:** a body moving along the ground: noise read at its speed through its modes (FoleyAutomatic), rolling far duller than sliding.
   - **Wind:** ambience at the listener: turbulent noise with gusts, its speed from the wind's log profile at the listener's height above the ground.
6. **The mixer** runs on the audio thread: at most 32 voices (the quietest gives way to a louder newcomer), a bus each for effects and ambience under a master volume, a peak limiter that looks 64 samples ahead and a soft clip last, so nothing clips. Real loudness spans 80 dB (wind at 35 dB, a log snapping at 130 dB a metre off) and a screen's speakers far less: the mix's window rides on the loudest sound begun (Frostbite's "HDR audio", DICE, GDC 2009), its top never below 70 dB SPL heard 6 dB under full scale and falling back over 2 s; the levels between sounds stay the physics'. The game thread works out each sound (its modes, gains, pulse) and sends it ready through a lock-free queue (`rtrb`); the audio thread only runs resonators (complex one-poles: accurate for low, long-ringing modes in f32) and filters, allocation-free once begun.
7. **Output:** kira with its cpal backend when there is a device; a **null output** when there is none (the reason printed) or the client is headless (screenshots, benches and CI never open it): the events are still gathered and counted. An **offline renderer** runs the same mixer into a WAV file: `creeer_client --sound <dir>` writes the reference sounds and a table of their measured peaks, decays and levels; `creeer_client --sound-play <dir>` plays the *log bridge* scene and records what its first builder hears through the game's own hooks.
8. **Volumes:** `creeer_audio::Volumes { master, effects, ambience }` (each 0–1, eased in over a few milliseconds), set on the planet with `PlanetView::sound.set_volumes(…)` and read with `PlanetView::sound.volumes()` (`crates/creeer_client/src/sounds.rs`), for the settings menu (stream A3).
9. **The hooks** in the client: `sounds.rs` (the frame; wind, breaks, footsteps), `sounds_bodies.rs` (the snapshots), `sounds_blows.rs` (own blows, fastenings), `sounds_things.rs` (how each thing sounds); in others' files only: `app/planet.rs` (one call each frame), `planet_view.rs` (the field), `animate/mod.rs` and `people.rs` (the feet as drawn), `main.rs` (the modes).

**Dependencies added** (both MIT or Apache-2.0): `kira` 0.12.6 without default features but `cpal` (it brings `cpal` 0.18, `atomic-arena`, `dasp_sample`, `mint`, `pastey`, `rtrb`, `triple_buffer`; on Linux `alsa`, on macOS `coreaudio-rs` and the `objc2` audio crates; its `glam` 0.34 is the workspace's own, and on Windows `windows` 0.62 was already built for wgpu), and `rtrb` 0.4 directly. The first build of `creeer_audio` with them and its other dependencies took 2 min 48 s on a machine shared with other agents' builds (the two not timed apart: the coordinator may time a clean `cargo build -p creeer_audio` on a quiet machine).

## Measured and rejected

| Variant | Measured | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| A struck body's radiation as a baffled piston, p = ρ₀ω·S·v/(2πr) | A 3 m log struck by a stone came to 128 dB at a metre in its fourth mode | Over a long body the faces' half-wavelengths cancel; the radiated power (decision 4) gives 108 dB, as hammering near the ear is measured |
| The ground's clicks (a heel, a stone landing) as an open-air dipole (second-order high-pass) | Footsteps on soil and rock at 23–25 dB SPL a metre off: inaudible | The ground's patch moves with what strikes it: a piston in a baffle (first order): 56–57 dB, as footsteps outdoors are |
| A click's expected loudness for the mix's window taken as its gain before its filters | The window's top set 30–45 dB too high by every soft heel: the footsteps scenes came out at −52 to −85 dBFS | The click's reach worked out from its contact and corner (`Click::reach`) |
| An impact as any change of velocity gravity does not explain (Δv − g·dt) | Every body at rest (held up against gravity every step) was "struck" 30 times a second: 3071 body sounds in 30 s of *log bridge* | A blow is a change beyond holding the body up (the length of Δv + g·dt, less g·dt): 246 in 45 s |
| A nail's ring decaying by steel's loss factor alone | T60 of the nail-driven scene 7.4 s | A nail's ringing leaks into the wood that holds it: damped by the wood's loss factor as well, T60 0.7 s |
| A real two-pole resonator (y = 2r·cos θ·y₁ − r²·y₂ + x) in f32 | — (chosen against before building: at 50 Hz and 48 kHz its coefficient rounds within 10⁻⁷ of 2) | The complex one-pole keeps low, long modes accurate in f32 at the same cost |

## Validation

The tests (`cargo test -p creeer_audio`: 37 tests; the client's hooks build and its 81 tests pass):

- **Modes against beam theory:** a struck 1 m steel bar, 25 mm square, rendered and measured by FFT: its first four peaks within 0.2 % of 132.9, 366.4, 718.2 and 1187.2 Hz (βₙ² ratios 1, 2.757, 5.404, 8.933). The mode shapes' mean squares are 1 within 10⁻⁶ and their ends ±2; Lamb's sphere roots are found from the spherical Bessel functions to 10⁻⁵; a nail's fundamental rises as 1/L² (four times from 50 to 25 mm standing; 0.9 kHz at 50 mm of a 3.1 mm nail).
- **Decays against the loss factor:** each clear mode of a struck spruce plank dies away within 5 % of T60 = 6.91/(π·f·η); a metal of η 0.001 rings 8 times as long as the spruce's 0.008, within 15 %.
- **Loudness against the impulse:** twice the impulse +6.0 dB, ten times +20.0 dB (within 0.1 dB); a firmer step louder; a burst of 30 or 300 grains carries one blow's energy (within a factor of 2, averaged over 40 seeds).
- **Contact:** Hertz's contact time of two 25 mm steel balls at 1 m/s within the measured 50–150 µs; a stone's contact on rock over ten times shorter than on soil (0.2 against 3.7 ms), its click over ten times louder; a short contact's click four times brighter (spectral centroid) than a long one's.
- **The mix:** twice the voice limit of 130 dB blows at once, three times: no sample at full scale, at most 32 playing; a sound 68.6 m off arrives 0.2 s later (within a block) and more than 10 times quieter; a whisper 300 m off beside a loud sound near is not begun; the ambience's volume at 0 silences the wind within 0.5 s.
- **Every reference scene** renders, is heard and never clips, and the struck bodies' lowest strong peaks stand at beam theory's modes.
- **The reference sounds' measurements** (`creeer_client --sound`, the table in `<frames>/sound/sounds.md`): log struck 67, 185, 363, 599 Hz (beam theory 67.1 for f₁), T60 1.5 s, 112 dB at a metre; steel bar 133, 366, 718, 1187 Hz, T60 9.1 s, 120 dB; plank dropped 66, 181, 355, 587 Hz (65.8); nail driven 151 Hz (the log), 399 Hz (the nail standing 75 mm out), T60 0.7 s, 110 dB; stone on rock 0.8 ms of click at 110 dB, on soil 11 ms at 89 dB; steps on soil, rock and gravel 56, 57 and 67 dB at a metre, on a green log 72 dB with its modes; a plank snapping 125 dB, a green log 131 dB.
- **The hooks, end to end** (`creeer_client --sound-play`, *log bridge*, 45 s, its first builder's ears): the builder's own nailing (5 blows), 70 footsteps, the crates landing and moving (246 sounds), the wind (4 changes); 85 sounds begun, 153 below the window or the threshold, none given way, the mix's peak −0.9 dBFS. The bridge's own break is not in it: the stringer left this builder's view at 9 s (what the server tells a client), so it is heard by those near it, which this capture is not.

## Cost

- **Audio thread** (release, best of three, a machine shared with other builds): 32 voices of 24 modes each with their clicks take 6.0–7.0 % of one core (a second of sound mixed in 60–70 ms). A test bounds it under 50 % in the lightly optimised test build (8–21 % measured there).
- **The frame** (`--sound-play`, debug build with dependencies optimised, 2698 frames of *log bridge*): gathering costs 3.8–6.8 µs a frame on average, 108–418 µs at worst (a frame when a fastening's blows are worked out and the crosshair's ray cast); loading the sound data and opening the output once, 1.5–2.5 ms on the planet's first frame. The coordinator should measure the window's frame on a quiet machine with a device (cpal's stream runs on its own thread).
- **The tick:** none (nothing runs on the server).

## Limits

Each also in `docs/future-improvements.md` (Sound).

- **Creaking is not heard in play:** the client is not told a member's stress. The synthesis (`happenings::creak`) and its data are built and tested and play in the reference scenes. *Protocol addition:* with each snapshot round, each structure in view with a member over half its strength: its most loaded members' indices and utilisation, a byte each (about 1 kbit/s while a bridge is loaded).
- **Which material a structure's member is:** not told; every member sounds as `hearing.ron`'s `structures` (green spruce). *Protocol addition:* each member's material index in `Kind::Structure` (a byte a member, once at spawn).
- **Which element broke when a structure comes apart:** not told (its members are replaced); the largest member gone cracks, at its middle. A broken joint (a nail) is not heard. *Protocol addition:* `Plastic`'s `broken` sent with the members replaced, or a `Broke { entity, element, at }` message.
- **Other players' blows and fastenings:** a `Swing` names no target, and the fastening's note goes to the fastener alone; others' blows are not heard. *Protocol addition:* the target (entity and point, about 12 bytes) with `Swing`, and a `Fastening { at, fastener, seconds }` to the clients near.
- **Impacts and landings of bodies the client predicts** are read from the server's snapshots, not its own contacts: heard when drawn, not when the prediction touches (within 67 ms). Contact impulses of bodies it does not predict are m·Δv between snapshots 33 ms apart: a blow and a rebound within one interval are one sound.
- **Shapes:** bodies are bars or balls of their volume; plates, shells, hollow crates and any FEM shape are not modelled (O'Brien 2002, James 2006). A compact body's spheroidal modes are left out (only Lamb's torsional ones).
- **Radiation** is an estimate (decision 4): no directivity, no near field, the ground's reflection left out; levels at a metre agree with published orders of magnitude, not measurements.
- **Estimates in the data:** the cracks' vibration share and grain counts, the creaks' onset, rate and energy, the grounds' grain counts and shift, the heel's mass and speed, the wind's level, the drivers' speeds and blows; each marked in its file.
- **No room acoustics or reverberation**, no HRTF (stereo panning only, no front from back), no Doppler, no occlusion by terrain.
- **Not synthesised:** water, fire, voices, creatures, music, cloth; lashing (no driver); digging (`Edits`), chopping a standing tree beyond a tool's blow, felling (a felled part's landing is heard).
- **The reference scenes' table** measures each scene's main sound alone; the WAVs carry the mix's window, so their levels are relative within each file.
- **`creeer_tools validate` does not check `data/sound/`** yet (the tests of `creeer_audio` do).
