# ADR-0027: Tools, reach and carrying, and the body's motion (G1.4)

**Status:** accepted (2026-10-22: G1.4 done, the user having played it; compliant hands at their ask). The user approved the plan (`docs/g1-plan.md`, G1.4) and chose, as recommended: the motion first, full finger bones, tools picked by number keys until G1.6's inventory, reach 2.5 m from the hands.
**Context:** `docs/g1-plan.md`, G1.4: the engineer's first work. He holds tools and strikes with them, reaches what the crosshair finds, carries and throws bodies. The user's first play (2026-10-22, after G1.3) asked for the motion to be better first: "the animation is very rough and it sometimes glitches, when running the player does not lean forward, also when stopping walking it does some more steps without moving, the arms look robotic, the fingers do not move and are always apart from each other: we can improve these things in 1.4".
**Builds on:** ADR-0022 (joints), ADR-0023 (the character: one rigid body, legs as a strut, balance within its strength), ADR-0025 (prediction of the bodies near the avatar), ADR-0026 (the engineer skinned on the ragdoll's fifteen bones; the gait from measurements, decision 9).
**Evidence:** G1.4a (the body's motion): `crates/creeer_client/src/animate/probe_tests.rs`, `animate_tests.rs`, the walk and run sheets of the style board; G1.4b (the hands): `animate/hands_tests.rs`, `hands.png`; G1.4c (tools): `art/tools.py`'s checks, `creeer_sim/src/tools.rs`, `world/looks_tests.rs`, `tools.png` and the icons; G1.4d (reach): `creeer_sim/src/reach.rs`, `world/reach_tests.rs`, `server/reach_tests.rs`; G1.4e (strikes): `creeer_sim/src/strike_tests.rs`, `world/strike_tests.rs`, `server/reach_tests.rs`; G1.4f (carrying): `world/carry_tests.rs`, `server/reach_tests.rs`, `joint/tests/motion.rs`; G1.4g: `play/work.rs`, `tests/scenarios/play.txt`.

## Where it starts

- **The motion** (`crates/creeer_client/src/animate/`): the gait from measurements (Sekiya's walk ratio, the walk-run change at Froude 0.5, Perry's rockers), feet planted and placed by inverse kinematics, the trunk the simulated body turned to its motion. The arms swing as one rigid piece each against the legs; the trunk does not lean; the gait runs on after the body stops until its phase ends; the hands are one bone each (the ragdoll's), the fingers fixed apart as modelled. A tool's swing has a hook (`Animator::swing`) and nothing behind it.
- **Reach and commands**: the F5 sandbox's commands act at up to 500 m; nothing is held.
- **Items**: none yet (G1.6). The crate is the only item model; tools have none.

## Decision

1. **The motion first** (G1.4a–b): the user's notes, each researched and checked in the game, before anything is held. The glitches are recorded and found before anything is smoothed over.
2. **Fingers as bones of the skin, posed by the client** (G1.4b): three bones per finger and the thumb, weighted in the art pipeline from the model's hands, posed procedurally (relaxed, gripping, open); the physics keeps its fifteen segments.
3. **Tools held before items exist** (G1.4c): the four tools modelled in Blender in the style (`art-style.md` §8, "Tools"), held in the right hand, picked by number keys from the sandbox's kit until G1.6's inventory replaces it.
4. **Reach 2.5 m from the hands** (G1.4d), checked by the server against what the client saw; the 500 m commands kept for creative and test servers.
5. **Strikes from a swing** (G1.4e): the strike's energy from the tool's mass and balance and the arm's strength, the swing drawn as it is simulated; its energy goes into what it hits (an impulse now; digging in G1.5, cutting in G1.7).
6. **Carrying and throwing** (G1.4f): a grab is a ball joint at the hands with a force limit from human strength; the load acts on the character, slowing and toppling it; a throw's speed from the object's mass, its reaction on the thrower.

### G1.4a: the body's motion

What the user saw, found first by a probe (`animate/probe_tests.rs`): one player over the real server and client, standing, walking at 1.4 m/s, stopping, running at 3.5 m/s, stopping, walking in a circle, jumping, walking back; every bone's turn between frames, relative to the pelvis. A glitch is a frame that turns much faster than the frames either side (a *spike*, rad/s over its neighbours). G1.3 had spikes up to 48.5 rad/s: setting off (the legs, 47), jumping (everything, 48.5), landing (38); after the run the stride never stopped.

- **Transitions inertialized** (`inertial.rs`, `pose.rs`): the pose (the pelvis in the frame of the feet, the joints' turns, the ankles in the world) of standing, stepping, the air and the water; a change between them draws the new pose at once plus the old one's offset decaying on Bollo's quintic over 0.2 s, from the old pose's velocity less the new one's (Holden). The new pose's velocity known a frame on, the old motion is carried that frame. Leaving the ground, the feet go with the body as it goes: a jump is a push the simulation gives at once.
- **The air found by the body's motion**: a leap (rising faster by half a jump's speed at once: the simulation's push comes in a tick, which nothing on the ground does), rising faster than half a jump's speed with the feet off the ground, or nothing within reach underfoot; in the air while it falls freely, taken to fall freely from the leap on. The legs drawn up at the top of a flight by (1 − (v/v₀)²)², v the body's rise as a body in flight rises from its leap: straight as they leave the ground and meet it again, drawing up and letting down from rest. Before, the feet stayed planted until 0.3 m clear and pinned the hips 30 cm below the body.
- **The motion measured as it is seen to change**: the body's velocity, rise and acceleration over the time since it last moved, so a body drawn faster than it ticks is not read as stop and go.
- **Each step keeps its gait** (`feet.rs`): a foot's share of the stride on the ground is the gait's as it landed, its lift as it lifted; a change of gait shows in the next step (people change over a stride). Before, the share changing under a foot as the speed passed 2.2 m/s lifted a planted foot 10 cm in a frame, or set a swinging one down.
- **Where a foot lands is settled through its swing** (Wang and Srinivasan) and it comes down where it was aimed. Before, it landed on the place computed in the landing frame, which moves fast as the gait or the speed changes: a planted sole slid 38 mm, and braking from a run the hips dropped 10 cm in five frames.
- **The hips on a smooth path** (Saunders, Inman and Eberhart; `hips.rs`): lowered at once as far as a planted foot needs, stepping with the knee never straighter than 5° (Perry's straightest stance knee; straighter, a knee's bend swings fast for a little reach, as the square root of the shortfall), and as far as a swinging foot will need when it lands, asked for more and more through its swing, from the hips as they will be then; coming back up as a mass, critically damped at 40/s (stepped exactly, the same at any frame rate).
- **Stopping**: braking, the stride keeps its time and its feet land where the body will be, no further than where it stops (v·t − ½·a·t²); the last steps are walked (both feet down between them), and the stride goes on until both feet stand where they would, then stands: a foot set down before the stop was asked for steps back in the same stride. Stepping into place starts with the foot furthest out. Setting off, the first foot lifts flat and rises from rest. Landing from the air, a stride begins anew.
- **The trunk leans** 7.3° running (Teng and Powers), and atan(a/g) setting off or speeding up, the push on the ground through the centre; braking, it stays upright (Harper et al.); about the hips, so the legs keep their reach; the head kept level.
- **The arms** (`upper.rs`): Murray's swing and elbow, between his two speeds by speed, the elbow following the shoulder by 0.05 s; running, the elbow about 90° (Yegian and Tucker) through fast walking's range; the change of carriage between walking and running over 0.15 s. Standing, the elbows at walking's least bend, 17°.

**After the user's play of G1.4a** (2026-10-22): "i can see the upper part of the player like 4 times because it flickeres evry frame; also when you stop walking it leans back a lot; also after jumping one leg end up in a bent position and the player keeps moving and coming to rest". The probe had drawn a frame a tick; the user's screen draws faster. Drawn at 144 frames a second it showed all three:
- the predicted avatar moving only as it ticks (ADR-0025, amended: now drawn between its ticks), and the motion measured frame to frame reading it as stop and go: spikes to 90 rad/s, the ways of moving changing back and forth;
- the trunk tilted back by the braking, 15° stopping from a walk, 21° from a run;
- a jump from standing set down at the top of its flight (the leap read as a fall slowing for a fifth of a second), its stride begun at the wrong foot, stepping in place for ever with the other leg folded behind (the knee at 107–118°), and stops ending a step short of standing (a foot 21 cm ahead of where it would stand), then stepping again.
The probe now draws at 60 and at 144 a second, a jump from standing, and gates each.

### G1.4b: the hands

- **Thirty finger bones in the skin** (`art/engineer/hands.py`): three a digit, `<digit>.<1–3>.<side>`, the thumb's from its metacarpal, the fingers' from their proximal phalanges, drawn only (the ragdoll keeps one segment a hand). The fingers are found on the posed skin: past the webs each finger is its own piece of the mesh (by connectivity, beyond the hand's length at which the hand parts into four), the thumb past the first web; each finger's centreline runs through its sections' centres. Each knuckle lies a finger's length (Buchholz) back from its tip, the middle joint halfway (Hamilton and Dunsmuir), the last at 1.3 : 1 (1 : 1 in the little finger); the thumb's joints by Buchholz's fractions, its base towards the wrist's radial side. The hand measures 200 mm from the wrist to the middle fingertip (ANSUR men's hand length 194 mm).
- **Weighted at the export** (`skinning.skin_groups`): each point's hand weight shared between the hand and the bones of the digit nearest it, by where it lies along the digit, blended across each joint (10 mm either side of a knuckle, 6 and 5 mm at the others, 15 mm at the thumb's base); every piece takes the shares of the skin point beneath it, as its other weights are. The skeleton check takes the thirty bones, all or none, each head within its hand's box.
- **Posed by the client** (`animate/hands.rs`): each digit's bones turned about their heads from the hand's frame, about each bone's axis across it towards the palm (the thumb towards the index). Relaxed as Lee et al. measured, the fingers laid beside the middle finger (the model's are spread); open, every joint straight and the fingers together. Gripping a handle across the palm (`Hold::Grip(radius)`, set by G1.4c's tools): the handle against the palm a radius back from the knuckles; all a finger's joints close together in proportion to their ranges, and a bone that meets the handle stops with the joints before it while the joints beyond go on; the thumb closes over the index finger's middle bone, its base as modelled. A grip is worked out once a radius. The hands are drawn so on the style board (`hands.png`: gloved and bare, relaxed and gripping 35 mm).

### G1.4c: tools in the hand

Each model's origin at its swing's grip, its handle along +y, its working end along +z.
- **Their masses from their volumes** (`tools.py`'s `report`, at every build): each part's closed volume times its material's density (steel 7850 kg/m³, hickory 800), the handle's share inside an eye taken off the steel by a Boolean intersection, a tube's as its surface times its wall; held to the listing's mass in `data/tools.ron` within 15 % (the build fails beyond): 3.01 kg (3.4 listed), 2.42 (2.2), 2.20 (2.1), 0.70 (0.743).

### G1.4d: reach

- **The reach** (`creeer_sim::reach`, the same for client and server): 2.5 m from the hands. The server's body never turns (its heading is the animation's), so the hands are measured from where they hang, on the body's axis: their centre standing, 0.79 m above the feet (0.38 m below the body's centre); the eye is the head's centre, 1.68 m up. The ground 2 m ahead of the feet is in reach, and a shelf 2.4 m up.
- **What the crosshair is on** (`client/aim.rs`, `planet_aim.rs`): the camera's ray against the bodies as the client draws them (their shapes from their spawns; a standing character its body's box; the player's own avatar left out) and the terrain; then the eye's ray to that point, and whatever it meets first is what is reached for. Walking, the crosshair is ringed when that is within reach and names it beneath ("box · 1.9 m"; "ground · out of reach" within 2 m more). Each thing aimed at carries the server tick it was drawn at: a body predicted with the avatar (ADR-0025) the avatar's own tick (the last snapshot it was set back to, and a tick for each input since), any other the tick the client draws at, a little in the past and fractional.
- **Judged as it was seen** (`world/reach.rs`, `world/history.rs`): each tick the server keeps where every body within 8 m of a player is, node by node (the reach, the hands' height, and the 3.5 m a running character covers in a second), for a second. A reach for a point on a body is judged on the body as it was at the tick seen (between the two kept ticks about a fractional one): the point must lie on its shape then, within 5 cm (the snapshots' quanta and the drawing between them); the point is then carried with the body to where it is now. A tick ahead of the server's (a predicted body: the client's inputs still wait in the jitter buffer) is judged on the body now, within 5 cm and as far as it can have gone in those ticks (its speed and spin), for at most the buffer's 6 ticks. Seen more than a second ago, or a body not near a player then, is refused. The point must be within reach of the hands now, by as far as the avatar can have run ahead (its speed over the buffer's 100 ms), and in the eye's sight past the ground (the ground's ray, the bodies left out: they may have moved since). A point on the ground must lie on it within 5 cm. Strikes (G1.4e) and grabs (G1.4f) are judged so. The server counts the client's ticks as its snapshots are numbered (`Server::world_tick`).
- **Creative and test servers** (`ServerWorld::creative`): the sandbox's commands (crates, balls, frames, machines, digging and filling within 500 m, clearing) are theirs alone; a player's look and tool are anyone's. The dedicated server is a survival one unless started with `--creative`; the planet sandbox in the client, the play harness, the stress scenes and the tests are creative. A loaded save keeps the server's setting.
- **Rays** (`creeer_physics::world::ray`): `Shape::ray` and `Shape::distance` (exact for balls and boxes; a hull's farthest face's plane), and the ground's ray alone, for the client and the server.

### G1.4e: strikes

- **The swing** (`creeer_sim::strike`): a tool's spin at impact ω = ω₀ (I₀ / I)^0.27, I its moment of inertia about the handle's end, ω₀ and I₀ the volunteers' 1 kg, 65 cm rod's (23.9 m/s at its end, mL²/3); its head's speed ω times its striking reach; its energy ½Iω². Each tool's I from its model (`art/tools.py` integrates every part's density over its volume, or a tube's over its surface, about the axis across the handle through its end): the table keeps its radius of gyration there and how far out it strikes, the build holding both to the model within 1 cm; the game's mass is the listing's. The axe: 21.3 rad/s, 17.3 m/s at its edge, 241 J; the pick 17.5 rad/s, 15.9 m/s at its point, 338 J; the shovel 352 J, the saw 74 J. Its downswing as long as the spin takes to build evenly through 120° (0.20 s for the axe, 0.24 for the pick); the blow falls 1 s after the swing begins (the raise the rest), and the next may begin 2 s after (30 strokes a minute).
- **The blow** (`world/strikes.rs`): a strike names what the player reached (G1.4d's checks) and the player's own tick when they asked; the swing begins there (the client predicts its avatar a few ticks ahead of the server, at the tick its inputs will be applied: so the blow falls at the tick its own swing strikes), never before the server's now and at most the jitter buffer's 6 ticks ahead. Its blow falls on the point as it has moved with its body, if still within reach of the striker standing; otherwise it misses. The impulse is the collision's, (1 + e) v / (1/Mₑ + 1/m), along the head's path (across the radius from the shoulders, in the plane of up and the point: forward overhead, down before the body, back towards the feet below), e the body's restitution, Mₑ the tool's effective mass at its striking point (Cross: 1/Mₑ = 1/M + b²/I_cm, b its striking reach less its balance; the axe 1.45 kg, the pick 2.54) and m the body's there (`World::effective_mass`: 1/m = 1/M + (r × d)·I⁻¹(r × d), its free axes alone). A blow on the ground keeps its energy for digging (G1.5); the blows of a tick are told (`take_blows`).
- **Everyone sees it** (protocol 10): `Command::Strike { target, started }` (G1.4d's `Target`), and `ServerMessage::Swing(who, at)`, the network tick its swing began, to every client that sees the striker; the striker's own client swings at once from its own tick. The right arm is raised overhead from its pose, struck down (the head's angle as the square of the time, its spin building evenly) and brought back (`upper::swing`, −1 to 2). Walking with a tool in hand, the left click strikes what the crosshair is on within reach.

### G1.4f: carrying and throwing

- **Held in both hands** (`world/carry.rs`, `creeer_sim::carry`): a grab names what the player reached (G1.4d's checks; not the ground, not another player) and puts away the tool in hand. A load the hands cannot lift at all (over 58.6 kg) is refused. The hands lift it to their height and then bring it in (not dragging it), at most with half the strength its weight leaves them (a 5 kg box from the ground in about 0.6 s, a 40 kg one in about 3 s), and hold it at the elbows' height (1.15 m up), a forearm (0.38 m) or as much farther as the load needs to clear the body's front by a centimetre, the way the body goes. The body turns no faster than it can still stop, the load swung round sped up (α·r) and pulled in (ω²·r) within that spare strength, and at most at the drawn body's 8 rad/s; the load turns with it.
- **Compliant hands** (`world/hands.rs`; the user, after playing the first carrying: "the boxes you pick up almost float when moving"): the arms are not a rigid hold. Each step the hands give the load its weight and the acceleration of their planned path (as people hold what they know the weight of), and pull it towards where they mean to have it with an arm's measured stiffness and damping: 445 N/m (the top of the endpoint stiffnesses measured in an active task, 45–445 N/m) at 0.47 of critical (Perreault and others: the human arm's more damped mode; 0.25 the less), softened for a light load to what the step can follow. The force is at most the 575 N the hands hold up; its turn is held to the way the body faces by a torque as strong as the grip (half the strength at each hand, 0.4 m apart). The same force goes back on the body at its centre. A load the hands give their most for, or that is pulled 0.3 m out of where they mean to have it, over a tenth of a second, drops (a 40 kg box with 30 kg set on it). The load passes the body's box while held (the box is no chest and arms: a load swaying in soft hands struck it and tumbled) and meets it again when let go. G drops it; a fall lets go.
- **Its weight on the body**: the hands' force is on the body's centre, its legs and balance take it (ADR-0023). Walking off with up to the most the hands lift, no load tipped the body over: the hands gave out first. The load slows the body's speeding up, not its walking speed (no measurement of that found).
- **Thrown** (`throw`, left click carrying): let go with the speed of an overarm throw of its mass from the thrower's own velocity, √(2A/(m + B)) (Cross: A 128 J, B a third of the forearm and hand's 2 kg): 6.7 m/s for 5 kg, a toss for 20; the thrower given its momentum back.
- **The physics' joints** (ADR-0022, amended; the first G1.4f held loads with a joint): a joint's force is what the step applied, its points may be moved with their velocities, the work of moving them is counted by the energy guard, and velocities the guard scales down are made to follow the moved points again; bodies let pass may be let collide again (`World::let_collide`).
- **Told to everyone** (protocol 13; 12 added the sandbox's light box, `Command::Box`: 0.4 m, 15 kg): `Command::Grab(Target)`, `Command::Drop`, `Command::Throw(direction)`; `Kind::Character { carries }`, the load carried (the arms drawn holding it: the upper arms hanging, the elbows a right angle, `upper::carrying`); the load told anew when taken and let go (a load in hand is not predicted: told once as a lone body, the client predicted it free inside the avatar, corrections of 2 m). The player's own load is drawn in its predicted hands: where it is from the avatar as both are drawn a little in the past, from the avatar as predicted (drawn in the past behind the predicted avatar, it trailed 0.34 m behind the hands walking over a 100 ms link). A load in hand when the world is saved is let go when it is loaded.

## Limits

- The swing knee bends to 78° walking (Perry about 60–65°): the foot's arc over its swing is not the measured one.
- A jump has no crouch before it: the simulation's push is at once, so the legs can only follow it (`docs/future-improvements.md`).
- Landing, the knees bend only as far as the hips are lowered to reach the ground; no absorbing crouch.
- Walking, the trunk is upright; people lean a little with speed when walking too, not measured here.
- The running arms are fast walking's swing about a 90° elbow: an extrapolation, not a measurement of runners' arms.
- A stop is unplanned (the input simply ends): a foot already set down ahead steps back after; from a run, standing takes 2.0 s.
- The player's own avatar is drawn up to a tick (17 ms) late, between its last two ticks.
- The thumb's joints and the fingers' lengths are Buchholz et al.'s fractions as recalled (the paper's table not to hand; the thumb's metacarpal checks with a CT survey, the knuckles with the webs).
- Gripping, the knuckles bend 30–46°, a little less than reported for cylinder grips (about 50–70°): the handle's place in the palm is set, not measured.
- In a fist, one skin point on the left thumb's web shows through the glove.
- The fingers turn only to bend (and to lie together); they do not spread, and the thumb's base does not move.
- Gripping, each finger's middle bone stands 6–15 mm off the handle (its tip meets the handle first and stops it; the bones are straight segments of one thickness, the finger's pads not modelled); people bear on a handle with every phalanx.
- A carried tool's handle slopes down behind about 30° (the neutral wrist's knuckle line); people carry an axe about level. Carried at its balance it hangs so without the wrist's effort; a small ulnar tip to level it is not measured here.
- The shovel's and the saw's heads' masses come from the models' volumes, not listings; the shovel models 10 % over its listing (a hickory handle's density on a handle that may be ash).
- The tools' masses are within 15 % of their listings, not exact; the pick's 3.01 kg against 3.4.
- A tool is held in the right hand only, and one at a time; it does not collide or weigh on the body until G1.4e–f; the left hand stays relaxed.
- The tool chosen is the sandbox's kit by number keys (G1.6's inventory replaces it); the choice is not saved with the player.
- Reach is measured from the hands' centre on the body's axis, not the right hand: the server does not know which way the body faces (up to about 0.2 m either way).
- The crosshair does not aim at structures' members or at a fallen character's pelvis yet (the frames' members and the pelvis's shape are not among the shapes the client aims at).
- The server's sight check is against the ground only: bodies in the way when the client saw it are not known to it (the client's own eye trace stops at them).
- Bodies more than 8 m from every player are not kept: one that came into reach faster than a running character (thrown or falling) within the second can be refused.
- The client does not know whether its server is creative; a refused sandbox command is only told back.
- The planet sandbox in the client is always creative (a survival game of one's own needs a choice when a game starts, not yet planned).
- The swing law is measured up to bats' swing-weights (about 0.2–0.3 kg·m² about the knob); the tools' (0.08–2.4) lie mostly beyond, where Cross and Bower saw the exponent grow: heavy tools may be swung a little slower than given.
- From the swing's start to its blow takes 1 s, half the measured cycle: how the cycle splits between the raise and freeing the tool is not measured.
- The swing is drawn with the right arm alone; an axe or a pick is swung with both hands.
- A blow is one impulse along the head's path: no friction along the surface, no edge biting in (G1.7 cuts), no injury to a person struck beyond being knocked down by the jolt.
- A structure's node is struck with its own mass and inertia: the members joining it to the rest are not counted in its effective mass.
- The striking client does not predict the blow's effect on the bodies it predicts: the struck crate moves when the server's word comes.
- A load slows only the body's speeding up: people walk slower with heavy loads in their hands, not measured here (no measurement found).
- A load is held at one point (where it was grabbed), its turn by a torque, not by two hands on its sides; how fast it is lifted (with half the hands' spare strength, up and then in) and turned is not measured. The arms' stiffness is the top of a measured range (an active task) and their damping ratio a measured one applied to the load they hold: neither is measured for carrying. The load's moment about the body is not on it (its weight and pull are, at its centre): given as the step's load, it set the body turning against its balance.
- Holding a load does not tire the hands (trainees held 31.8 kg at their elbows for 53 s on average): it is held until dropped or let go.
- Standing still, the drawn body faces the way it last walked, which may not be the load's way until it walks.
- The client's prediction of its avatar knows nothing of the load (its weight on the body): corrected rarely, as measured.
- The throw is Cross's one-handed overarm law for 57 g to 3.4 kg, used for heavier loads thrown with both hands.

## Amended in G1.6c (2026-10-22): the hands move the arms too

Picking up a 14 g stone (ADR-0029), the hands planned its lift to elbow height in two ticks (the strength over so small a mass) and tore it from their hold. The lift's acceleration is now half of what the hands' strength leaves over the load's weight, shared by the load and both arms (`carry::spare`; the arms 4.94 % of a man's mass each, de Leva 1996): a pebble rises in about 0.8 s, a 15 kg box a little slower than before. A load whose lift time is zero (already where the hands hold it) no longer divides 0 by 0 (`hands.rs`, found in G1.6a).

## Amended after the user's play of G1.6 (2026-10-23): pulled at the centre of mass

Carried sticks spun up to 33 rad/s as the player walked: the hands pulled the load at the point they gripped, a stick's grip lies off its axis, and it barely resists turning about that axis, so the pull's lever drove it. The hands now pull at the load's centre of mass, towards where it is when the grip is where they mean it and the load turned as they keep it, and hold its turn as an angular spring of one natural frequency on every axis through its full inertia (the torque its inertia tensor times the angular acceleration asked), softened to ring at most half a step: stable however unequal its moments. ## Amended in G1.7 (2026-10-23): the fist holds a handle's own axis

Found by a test walking a stick through the forest (ADR-0031): a 1.2 m stick carried into a trunk, or a crate, spun about its own axis to 450–525 rad/s and was dropped. Its moment about that axis is a six-hundredth of the others: the contact's friction, pressed by the hands' pull, turned it, and the arms' spring (stable at half a step's ringing on the largest moment) cannot hold an axis so light. A fist closed round a handle holds that axis by the grip's friction: after each step, a slender load's spin about its least axis (under a tenth of its largest moment), relative to the hands' turning, is taken away up to what the grip's torque (57.5 N·m) gives over a step, as a Coulomb impulse: it only removes energy (`hands.rs`, `grip_holds_twist`). Tested: the stick into a trunk or a crate stays under 6.4 rad/s and in the hands (`trees_tests.rs`).

## Amended in G2.9 (2026-10-26): raising a long thing by one end

The user's play: "how to dig it into the ground?" A carried load kept the turn it was taken with, so a log lifted lying stayed lying, and no post could be stood in its hole by hand.

**Prior work.** One person raises a pole or post by setting its foot against something (a hole's wall, a stake, a foot), lifting the top end overhead, and walking towards the foot, hands moving along it, until it stands. Linemen "pike" poles up the same way with several people. Games that stand posts place them upright at once (Valheim, Rust); none we know of raises them by hand.

**Decision** (`raising.rs`):
- What the hands cannot hold level is held by one point. The test: a lone node (a log, a pole, a plank) whose weight times its lever about the grip is more than the grip's torque (`hands::grip`, 50 N·m). That point is on its long axis.
- **No torque:** it turns as it will about the hands, the other end resting where it lies.
- **The force** is the arms' (stiffness 445 N/m, damping, strength as for carrying), plus the share of its weight a beam resting on its far end puts at the hands: its centre's lever about that end over the hands', across the vertical.
- **The plan:** the hands rise to about head height before the body (0.6 m above the elbows). The plan advances only while the end keeps within 10 cm of it: the hands wait for a heavy end. Planned on regardless, the end fell behind out of the arms' reach and dropped.
- **Hand over hand:** the hands move along it at up to 0.5 m/s towards the point nearest where they mean to be, so walking towards its foot raises it.
- **Weight:** raising by one end needs strength for half its weight. Lifting it whole would need all of it.

Validation (`raising_tests.rs`):
- A 3 m green spruce pole (27 kg) grabbed at its end is held by that point and raised to 20–80° from the vertical.
- A crate is still held level.
- Walked under with its foot over a 30 cm hole, the pole comes up to 22° from the vertical (gate 30°) with its foot 47 cm down the hole (gate 20 cm), at 0.45 m/s at most.
  - At first it came up to 44° with its foot 24 cm down, and then, pressed against the hole's rim, was thrown at 1,254 m/s: the energy guard stopped it dead, and the next step exploded.
  - That was the terrain's gradient on the hole's axis (central differences across its ridge) and a redone step that lost the guard's books, not the raising: fixed in ADR-0005 ("Amended in G2.9: a rod against a narrow hole's rim"). The raising controller is unchanged.
