# The physics, reviewed against the state of the art (2026-10-18)

Written at the user's request after P1's late survey (ADR-0024): a recapitulation of the physics as built from F2 to G1, and its systemic weaknesses, each set against how the literature and other engines handle the same problem. A weakness here is a property of the architecture that keeps producing bugs, slowness or patches, not a single defect.

## 1. What is built

### The core: one implicit minimisation per group (ADR-0004, ADR-0005)

- **Bodies.** Structures are 3D co-rotational Timoshenko beams on nodes with 6 degrees of freedom (ADR-0004 Addendum 1); a rigid body is a structure of one node. Plasticity is a convex elasto-plastic potential at the members' ends (N–M interaction), with hardening, strain rate (Cowper–Symonds), buckling by splitting members, and fracture by plastic strain.
- **The step.** Each group of touching or joined bodies minimises one incremental potential: inertia, elastic and plastic energy, contacts, friction, joints, struts, dashpots. Newton with L-BFGS on a factorised matrix (members' own sparse Cholesky, rigid bodies' joint sparse Cholesky; deterministic, bit-identical across platforms), a backtracking line search whose first trial stops where an open contact closes, a stop from the estimated remaining error (0.3 µm), at most three refreshes of lagged quantities.
- **Integrators, chosen per group and per step.** Newmark (average acceleration) for free groups; BDF2 for groups with contacts, stopped joints or held bodies; backward Euler for impact steps. An **energy guard** checks every step: a gain beyond its slack redoes the step with a more dissipative scheme, and as a last resort takes the excess from the kinetic energy. Energy that contacts found anew hold beyond what the last step left in them is removed too.
- **Substeps.** The whole world in up to 16 substeps while any box turns faster than 0.05 rad a step, and the step redone if a box turned more; a group in up to 8 while its joints turn fast; a struck structure in 0.5 ms impact substeps while it flows plastically.

### Contacts (ADR-0005, amended through F6)

- Found once at the start of each (sub)step, with a speculative margin of twice the step's motion; normals and points fixed for the step, except normals that turn with a body or with two edges.
- **Normal laws:** Hertz points from the materials, face patches as a flat punch in series with the resolvable stiffness, a load-stiffening cap, dashpots on the gap's own rate.
- **Friction:** anchored stick springs and Coulomb sliding, on normal forces lagged from the last refresh; drilling friction for balls.
- **Impacts:** Newton's restitution as impulses outside the minimisation: an inelastic pre-stop before the step, the rebound after it, capped by the energy before the step less the plastic work.

### Joints, struts, characters (ADR-0022, ADR-0023, ADR-0024)

Joints in maximal coordinates as augmented-Lagrangian terms of the minimisation (k = 100·m/β², refreshed with the lagged quantities), a velocity projection after each substep, per-group joint substeps. Struts (one-sided capped springs) carry a character's legs. Ragdolls are fifteen boxes on fourteen such joints.

### Around it

Sleeping by groups; simulation LOD (parking, ADR-0019); the air's and the sea's forces put on before the step and taken off after it (ADR-0014); drivetrains (ADR-0012), circuits (ADR-0011), heat (ADR-0010), pipes (ADR-0014) and chemistry (ADR-0013) as their own solvers, coupled at the step's midpoint with books that close (ADR-0016); orbits by IAS15 (ADR-0007).

### Its strengths, which the review does not question

- **Accuracy pinned by tests** against analytic and published references (validation tables in every ADR), converged steps, not truncated ones.
- **Determinism** across platforms by construction (own sparse solvers, fixed summation order, `creeer_math::det`).
- **One unified energy formulation** for bodies, contacts and structures, which made struts and joints simple to add.
- **Books that close** in every domain (energy, mass, charge).

## 2. Systemic weaknesses

Each: what it is; the evidence in this project's history; what others do; the direction.

### W1. The time integrator is a patchwork around an energy guard

**What.** Three schemes switched per group and per step (Newmark, BDF2, backward Euler), with history resets at impacts, impulses outside the minimisation, an energy guard that redoes or damps steps, and a surplus rule for energy contacts found anew.

**Evidence.** A large share of the physics bugs have been interactions between these pieces:
- the guard measuring turning contacts before they were set up (21 J found every step: ADR-0024);
- the guard ratcheting joints' bounded energy wobble, and killing drives (ADR-0022);
- BDF2 losing 0.6 % of a burn's Δv; momentum kept only when equal and opposite loads are stepped under one scheme (`Body::held`, ADR-0023);
- impacts that travel through a pile within a step needing the whole group to be backward Euler (ADR-0024);
- the trapezoidal rule's energy gain in nonlinear impacts (×4.5 in one tick, ADR-0004), the reason the guard exists.

**What others do.** Nonsmooth mechanics treats impacts and smooth motion within one scheme instead of switching:
- **Moreau–Jean time-stepping** (event-capturing, velocity-level constraints) is convergent and robust for many impacts; Acary and Collins-Craft (JTCAM 2025) give it an impact law with friction after Frémond whose discrete energy balance has non-negative dissipation by construction.
- **The nonsmooth generalized-α scheme** (Chen, Acary, Virlez, Brüls 2013; Brüls, Acary, Cardona, CMAME 2014) splits the impulsive part (first order) from the smooth part (generalized-α, second order with controllable damping) and enforces constraints at both position and velocity levels, solved by one semismooth Newton per step.
- For the smooth part, **energy-momentum and energy-decaying integrators** (Simo & Tarnow 1992; Gonzalez; Armero & Romero) conserve or dissipate energy by construction where Newmark can blow up.

**Direction.** One integrator for every group: a smooth part with controllable numerical dissipation that cannot gain energy, and an impulsive part handled within the same solve. The guard becomes a check that never fires, not a mechanism. This is the largest change in this review (an ADR of its own); it would remove the scheme switches, the history resets and most of the guard's special cases.

### W2. Contacts are penalties on a contact set frozen for the step

**What.** Penetration is allowed (by design: Hertz and face laws from the materials), but the contact set is found once per step and its geometry frozen, and the laws carry layers of corrections: the resolvable stiffness, the load-stiffening cap, the punch in series, dashpots on the gap's own rate, measured depths on curved ground, turning normals.

**Evidence.** World-wide turn substeps (up to 16) and turn redos exist because a frozen manifold lets corners pass into neighbours; a fast-spinning hand made the whole world substep (ADR-0024). Contacts found anew differ from those the last step ended with (the surplus rule, rattling between edge and face contacts, P1's list). Edge contacts measured 6 mm apart and 4 mm deep at once.

**What others do.**
- **Incremental Potential Contact** (Li et al. 2020; Rigid-IPC, Ferguson et al. 2021; Affine Body Dynamics, Lan et al. 2022) keeps the contact set alive within the minimisation: continuous collision detection bounds every line-search step, so no configuration ever intersects and no substeps are needed for turning; the cost is higher, and rigid rotations are hard to trace (ABD replaces them with stiff affine bodies for that reason).
- **MuJoCo** relaxes contact into a convex, compliant model (and Drake similarly), solved by Newton in generalized coordinates in few iterations.
- **Complementarity formulations** (Signorini–Coulomb, NCP) are exact for rigid contact; ADMM and proximal solvers (Le Lidec, Carpentier et al., 2024) handle ill-conditioning from mass ratios.
- Games' engines (Unity's speculative CCD, Jolt) accept ghost contacts and occasional tunnelling for speed.

**Direction.** Keep the material laws (they are the realism), but let the contact set and its geometry follow the iterate: re-detect or extend contacts within the step (bounded by the line search, as IPC's CCD does), and make contacts persistent across steps with feature identities, so that a contact is the same contact from one step to the next (its energy, its anchor, its multiplier). This removes the world-wide turn substeps and the found-anew surplus.

### W3. Friction rests on lagged normal forces, refreshed at most three times

**What.** Friction's normal force is lagged and refreshed only where the minimisation converges, at most three times a step.

**Evidence.** 19 % of a rock pile's steps end with friction on a normal force more than 5 % off its own (ADR-0017); 43 % of a ragdoll's iterations come after refreshes (ADR-0024); piles creep and rattle.

**What others do.** IPC lags friction too, and its authors note sticky artifacts at large steps and high friction. The contact-mechanics standard is **Alart and Curnier's augmented Lagrangian** (1991): normal and tangential multipliers solved together by a (semismooth) Newton method, locally superlinear (Kunisch & Stadler 2005 for its analysis). A 2024 barrier-augmented Lagrangian method (arXiv 2407.00046) updates friction within each inexact Newton iteration and reports fewer iterations than lagging.

**Direction.** Friction as a semismooth Newton (Alart–Curnier) term of the step, not a lagged outer loop. ADR-0017's coupled variant C is the first half of it, and it showed why the line search must then measure the consistent problem, not the lagged one.

### W4. Joints are penalties in maximal coordinates

**What.** Joints hold only to their penalty and refreshes (1 µm or 1 % of their force), need a velocity projection and substeps, and make a ragdoll fifteen bodies.

**Evidence.** The joints ask for 93 % of a ragdoll's refreshes; a ragdoll step costs 13 Newton iterations; mass ratios of 300 between segments (ADR-0024).

**What others do.** Reduced coordinates for jointed trees (Featherstone's methods, PhysX articulations, MuJoCo): exact joints, a sixth of the unknowns, large mass ratios tolerated; RedMax (Wang et al. 2019) mixes them with maximal-coordinate constraints where loops close, in one implicit step.

**Direction.** As ADR-0024 now plans: ragdolls (and later machines' trees) as articulated bodies in reduced coordinates within the step; ADR-0022's joints kept for loops and grabs.

### W5. Newton is not quite Newton

**What.** The matrix is Gauss–Newton (the contacts' turning terms and the joints' geometric stiffness left out), reused across iterations with L-BFGS for structures, rebuilt per iteration for rigid groups; the line search cuts each iteration short where an open contact closes.

**Evidence.** Linear convergence near the end (44 % of a pile's iterations within a thousand tolerances of it, ADR-0017); a third of a pile's iterations cut short at closing contacts; B1's exact Hessian alone gained only 7 % (because the lag dominated).

**What others do.** A 2023 study of Newton solvers for incremental potentials ("Pitfalls of Projection", arXiv 2311.14526) finds unconditional positive-semidefinite projection slows convergence and proposes projecting on demand (PDN) or a kinetic regularisation instead, with an acceleration-based stopping rule and a parameter-free line search.

**Direction.** Revisit the exact Hessian with projection on demand once friction is exact (W3), since then the problem Newton sees is the problem it solves.

### W6. One heavy group runs on one core

**What.** Groups step in parallel; a single group's iterations are serial (fine-grained parallel regions within an iteration gain at most 1.5× here, `rayon_bound_tests.rs`). No SIMD kernels, no GPU path.

**What others do.** Distributed ABD (2026) splits a minimisation of hundreds of bodies and more by consensus ADMM with an adaptive per-body penalty; Peiret et al. (2019) substructure stiff multibody systems through the Schur complement; AVBD (2025) and VBD trade convergence for GPU parallelism.

**Direction.** As planned: SIMD in P2; consensus ADMM for large groups (piles) in P1; nothing fine-grained.

### W7. Couplings put on before the step and taken off after it

**What.** Several couplings are explicit: the air's and the sea's forces and dashpots (pushed and settled around the step), slow networks a coupling interval late, and until P1 the characters' legs.

**Evidence.** The legs' spring on a light body broke the explicit stability limit (k·h² below the mass) and was the play scene's 58 ms tick (ADR-0024).

**What others do.** Explicit co-simulation is known to leak or create energy at its interfaces and to destabilise for stiff couplings (Benedikt et al., NEPCE 2013; Multibody System Dynamics 2019 and 2022 on energy errors in explicit co-simulation); implicit coupling, or a stability criterion per interface, is the remedy.

**Direction.** Audit every explicit coupling against its stability criterion (a spring: k·h² ≪ m; a dashpot: c·h ≪ m; added mass against the body's mass), and move those that can fail it into the minimisation, as the struts did.

### W8. The world is rebuilt from scratch each step

**What.** Contacts, groups and their geometry are found anew each step; what carries over is kept in side tables (anchors, the energy left in contacts, joints' multipliers) matched by bodies and points.

**Evidence.** Most consistency bugs lived in these side tables or in the gap between measuring and solving: the found-anew surplus, and the guard measuring the contacts before the step had set them up (ADR-0024).

**What others do.** Real-time engines keep contact manifolds persistent across steps with feature identities, for warm starting and consistency (Box2D, Jolt's contact cache); IPC keeps its constraint set alive within a step.

**Direction.** Persistent contacts (W2) carry their own state; the side tables go.

### W9. The tests see too few configurations

**What.** Validation is strong analytically, but most scenes are axis-aligned and unturned, single scenarios, often at rest.

**Evidence.** The turning bug lived from F5 to P1 because most tests' bodies are unturned; chaotic single runs misled measurements (ADR-0017's and ADR-0024's lessons).

**Direction.** Property tests with random orientations and positions for every contact and joint law (energy found against energy left, gradients against differences); benches as families of cases and fixed recorded states (as `ragdoll_bench.rs` now does); an energy audit per step in debug builds that names the term that gained.

## 3. What to do, in order

| | What | Weaknesses | Size | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Property tests with random orientations; an energy audit per step | W9 (and finds W1, W2, W8 bugs early) | Small | Now, before more solver work |
| 2 | Audit of the explicit couplings, each against its stability criterion | W7 | Small | Now |
| 3 | Ragdolls as articulated bodies in reduced coordinates | W4 | Large | P1 (ADR-0024) |
| 4 | Friction by a semismooth Newton (Alart–Curnier) in the step | W3, W5 | Large | P1 |
| 5 | Persistent contacts, their set following the iterate | W2, W8 | Large | P1–P2 |
| 6 | One nonsmooth integrator (impulsive and smooth parts in one solve), the guard a check | W1 | Very large | Its own package; an ADR with a survey first |
| 7 | Consensus ADMM for large groups; SIMD kernels | W6 | Large | P1 (piles), P2 |

Each is a design, so each starts with its own survey (plan.md §1, principle 12) and its own ADR; this review only points the way.
